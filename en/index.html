Challenge

Interest peaks when no one is there to answer it

  • Buyers browse cars in the evenings, at night and on weekends — outside dealer opening hours.
  • Interest is highest at the exact moment a salesperson or trade-in manager is unreachable.
  • Price negotiation easily stalls waiting for manual approval.
  • That delay can break the buying decision entirely.
  • Kopilotti Sales gives the customer an immediate, controlled next response.

The goal is to shorten the time between a customer's offer and the dealer's response.

Researched buying behaviour

The buyer is already online when the deal gets decided

The used-car buying journey most often starts online. 90% of buyers research online, and price influences the choice for 72%. Almost a third are ready to complete the purchase digitally. Yet price negotiation still often waits for a salesperson or trade-in manager.

  • 90% research the car online
  • 72% say price influences the choice
  • Almost 1/3 complete the purchase digitally

Source: Car-buying process study 4/2026, Alma Mobility, Nettiauto and Autotalli, n=712 (Finland).

Kopilotti Sales brings this last manual step into the same digital buying journey.

Normal car retail

Digital price negotiation doesn't turn car retail into an auction

Not an auction. Not dynamic pricing. A digital price negotiation within the market price.

Kopilotti Sales is intended for the normal retail sale of used vehicles. It is not an auction, a bidding competition, or an automated clearance channel.

The vehicle is still sold at the market price and on the same terms the dealer has set, as in ordinary car retail. Digital price negotiation only operates within the dealer's own pre-defined business rules.

Before negotiating, the customer has access to the listing and the dealer's own condition report. The sale is ordinary consumer retail, subject to consumer-protection rights the same way as any other dealer consumer sale.

Digital price negotiation doesn't change the terms of the sale. It only removes unnecessary waiting when a decision can be made safely within the dealer's business rules.

Process

How it works

  1. 1

    Customer selects a vehicle

    Kopilotti Sales opens per vehicle, from a listing page or the dealer's existing sales flow.

  2. 2

    Customer starts the price conversation

    The digital salesperson holds a natural conversation with the customer and captures an offer.

  3. 3

    The system checks dealer-defined boundaries

    The commercially critical decision is made server-side. Price boundaries are never stored in the browser or left for a language model to decide.

  4. 4

    Customer receives a response

    The outcome can be an accepted offer, a controlled counter-offer, ending the negotiation, or escalating the case to the dealer.

Transaction layer

Price decisions belong in the digital journey, before checkout

E-commerce already handles vehicle discovery, identification, contracting and payment. Kopilotti Sales adds the missing step: the customer's offer, a bounded negotiation, and a deterministic price decision.

Current digital path

  1. Vehicle selection
  2. Condition report
  3. Fixed online price
  4. Identification
  5. Contract
  6. Payment

Kopilotti Sales

  1. Vehicle selection
  2. Condition report
  3. Offer + negotiation
  4. Deterministic price decision
  5. Checkout
  6. Contract
  7. Payment

Kopilotti Sales forms the agreed price and hands the customer to the dealer's own checkout, contract and payment process. Funds move directly from the customer to the dealer. Kopilotti does not receive, hold or transfer funds. The dealer confirms payment.

Trade-in and settlement

Trade-in is part of the same deterministic transaction decision

The Trade-in V1 target flow handles vehicle identification, VIS valuation, a dealer-selected vehicle-history check, an offer, and settlement as separate, traceable steps.

  1. 1Registration and odometer reading
  2. 2Vehicle identification
  3. 3VIS valuation
  4. 4Optional history check
  5. 5Deterministic offer
  6. 6Settlement and deal summary

VIS / Autovista

Integration boundary built, no production connection

The trade-in flow can process an estimate supplied by an external valuation service, but there is no active VIS / Autovista connection.

  • The internal flow is tested with a test provider; an actual VIS connection has not been implemented or tested against the service.
  • A missing or failed valuation is routed to manual review — the system never guesses a value.
  • Going live requires a licensed agreement, documentation, credentials, sandbox verification and contract testing.

Optional vehicle history / carVertical

Dealer-selected supporting input for pricing

In the target flow, the dealer may request a history check after the VIS estimate to support pricing and the commercial decision.

  • A provider-independent risk layer and preliminary carVertical adapter have been implemented and unit-tested on a separate development branch.
  • The adapter assumptions have not been confirmed with carVertical, and the code has not been run against the service's sandbox or production environment.
  • The history service does not set the trade-in price: its findings are handled by dealer rules or a person. The optional step has not yet been wired into runtime in this target order.

Production boundary: Trade-in V1, DealSnapshot and the vehicle-history risk layer are implemented and tested in separate implementations, but they are not yet connected to this public demonstrator's production traffic. There is no announced VIS / Autovista or carVertical partnership or active production integration.

Distinction

What sets Kopilotti Sales apart from a chatbot

Typical chatbot

  • Answers general questions
  • Routes to customer service
  • Retrieves information from a knowledge base
  • Does not participate in price decision logic

Kopilotti Sales

  • Operates inside a specific vehicle's purchase context
  • Runs a bounded price negotiation
  • Uses commercial boundaries the dealer has defined
  • Can continue the conversation outside sales-staff hours
  • Escalates exceptions to a person

Kopilotti Sales does not need to replace the dealer's existing chat solution — it handles one bounded, commercially critical moment.

Lightweight onboarding

A pilot starts with a single link

Kopilotti Sales can go live without a multi-week integration project. The dealer adds one Negotiate the price link to a vehicle page, and deeper integrations are built only once the model has proven its value.

  1. 1

    Vehicle-specific magic link

    The link carries only a random, opaque token, for example sales.kopilotti.online/n/8F3KD91X. Vehicle data and negotiation rules are fetched from the server — a floor or target price is never added to the URL.

  2. 2

    Kopilotti Admin

    The dealer sets the list price, the lowest acceptable price, counter-offer steps, campaigns and a validity window. Admin generates the link and a ready-to-use HTML snippet for the vehicle page.

  3. 3

    API and integrations

    Once the model is validated, link creation can be automated through DMS, e-commerce and CRM integrations, and through marketplaces' own APIs and partnership terms.

Pilot workload for the dealer: adding one link to a vehicle page. No mandatory DMS, CRM or marketplace integration.

Control

The dealer sets the negotiation boundaries. Kopilotti Sales operates only inside them.

A dealer can define negotiation terms, for example:

  • Per vehicle
  • Per vehicle group
  • Per price band
  • Based on a campaign
  • Based on stock position
  • As requiring manual approval

The model used is agreed during the pilot, matched to the dealer's own pricing process.

Safety and control

AI can support the conversation. Verified rules and provider states control the transaction.

  • The language model does not decide price boundaries.
  • The dealer's rules are processed server-side.
  • The browser must never know internal boundaries.
  • Exceptional cases can be routed to a person.
  • The customer's identity is verified before the actual price negotiation begins.
  • The number of automated negotiation rounds is limited, and the negotiation moves to the dealer when needed.
  • Commercial decisions and their basis are recorded traceably.
  • An unclear or incomplete situation does not automatically result in a price promise.
  • The dealer retains control over pricing.
  • The system does not independently grant credit or make financing decisions; financing applications are intended to be handled by the financing provider, in the provider's own environment.
  • The service does not receive, hold or transfer customer funds.
